# Atlas of Objectivism: data pipeline

The viewer is `public/atlas/` (installable as an app: see `PWA.md`) (plain three.js, no build step) and is the source of truth: edit
`public/atlas/main.js` / `index.html` directly. This folder holds what produces its data.

```
D1 (whoneedsit) --export_whoneedsit.{R,py}--> atlas/data/whoneedsit_export.json
                --make_whoneedsit.{R,py}----> public/atlas/data/whoneedsit.json   (what the viewer loads)
```

Run everything from the repo root.

| Step | R | Python |
|---|---|---|
| Re-pull the four tables from D1 (needs `CF_ACCOUNT_ID`, `CF_API_TOKEN` with D1 read; optional `D1_DATABASE_ID`) | `Rscript atlas/export_whoneedsit.R` | `python atlas/export_whoneedsit.py` |
| Rebuild the viewer data (buildings, layout, people, routes, highlight) | `Rscript atlas/make_whoneedsit.R` | `python atlas/make_whoneedsit.py` |

The R and Python makers produce the same buildings, assignments and routes (only random positions differ).
Credentials come from the environment only; do not commit them. The SQL is in `atlas/sql/` and selects named
columns only (no contact fields).

Dependencies: `atlas/requirements.R` (R: pacman, httr2, jsonlite) or `atlas/requirements.txt` (Python: numpy).

## Legend: what each kind of data looks like in the Atlas

| D1 source | Atlas representation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `orgs` (active or not) | Building, one floor per 6 people (min 1) | Defunct orgs stay visible until the Time Machine exists. Peikoff orgs (`PEIKOFF_ORGS`) are red. |
| `people` | Walker (person) in their primary org's building; no org = on the sidewalks | Feminine first names get long hair. Peikoff people are red. |
| `relationships` | Which building a person belongs to; 2+ orgs = a walking route | Doors and elevators are used to enter and leave. |
| `websites` (live) | Fish looping round the stream | Color by kind: gold org, orange Substack, teal personal, green forum/blog, pink store, purple publication. |
| `websites` (defunct) | Pale skeleton fish, slower | Card links only to the archive copy. |
| `social` groups / communities | Cottage; size = members | Mountainside: Facebook, LinkedIn, Goodreads, Google Groups, Reddit (highest). Gulch floor: Meetup. Door color = platform; roof color varies (red only for Peikoff properties). |
| `non_objectivist_orgs` | Not drawn | General universities with no specific Objectivist group. |
| `namesakes` | Planned: drawn differently from `orgs` buildings | Small businesses that borrowed the movement's names. Not read by the pipeline yet. **J. Galt Finance Suite** (c. 2021-2024, defunct) is recorded here and is still drawn as a building through `orgs.id` 181 until the pipeline reads `namesakes`; new namesakes (e.g. from Octoparse scrapes) will get their own look. |
| Scenery only (no data row) | Mountains, lake, stream, the Atlantis Railway (a freight railway line with a tunnel and one freight train), Easter eggs (Kira Peikoff, pirate ship for theVault, "City Forum" lab) | Not searchable or selectable as data. |

## Mapping rules (constants at the top of the makers)

- Every org is a building; one floor per 6 people (min 1); a person stands in their primary org's building, people with no org on the sidewalks.
- Primary org: a founder / CEO / president / chair / owner link wins (best-verified; ARI wins ties), else org 1 (Ayn Rand Institute) if the person has any link to it, else real roles over authorship/grantee links, then better-verified; `PRIMARY_OVERRIDE` pins individuals (Amy Peikoff -> Don't Let It Go).
- Layout: ARI in the center cell; `PEIKOFF_ORGS` in the cells around it; other orgs with >= 5 people spread as far apart as possible; the rest fill in; `NEXT_TO` keeps a pair adjacent. The street grid is what the walkers follow.
- People linked to 2+ orgs get a route (`routes`) and walk between those buildings; `ROUTES_EXTRA` adds destinations by hand (it is currently unused for Barbara Branden Peikoff, who stands and talks instead: see `MEET`).
- `HIGHLIGHT`: people whose name contains "peikoff", plus ids in `ids` (Barbara Branden Peikoff), and `PEIKOFF_ORGS` are drawn red whatever the color setting. Only Peikoffs are red (Ayn Rand is not; she is invited to `/salon` through `SALON_EXTRA`); the viewer also drops anyone from the red group whose name does not contain "Peikoff" (`peikoffsOnly` in `public/atlas/search.js`).
- `DISPLAY_NAMES` / `ALIASES`: Poso Press is shown as Postsocialist Press and still found by "Poso".
- `EXCLUDE_ORGS`: orgs removed from the city (no building; their link and relationship rows are dropped, their people stay via other orgs or on the street). Currently org 55 (Critics) and general universities with no specific Objectivist group: 33, 34, 36, 37, 38, 44, 47, 48, 49, 50, 54, 107. Universities stay only through a specific group (Salem Center, Civitas Institute, Hoover Institution, Snow Institute, SPPC) unless the university itself is Objectivist.
- Org links (`urls` in the export): `orgs.url` / `url2` plus `websites` rows for the org, minus defunct sites; the building card lists up to 3 (org site first, Prometheus grantee page and Wikipedia last).
- General universities (no specific Objectivist group) now live in the D1 table `non_objectivist_orgs` (same ids they had in `orgs`; 12 rows: Bowling Green, Carnegie Mellon, Duke, George Mason, Harvey Mudd, Seton Hall, Maryland, Pittsburgh, UT Austin, Wisconsin Madison, Columbia, Francisco Marroquin), and their 11 faculty/grantee rows moved to `non_objectivist_org_relationships`. Groups inside a university (Hoover, Civitas, ...) stay in `orgs`. The export mirror no longer lists them; `EXCLUDE_ORGS` keeps the ids as a guard.
- `OUTSKIRTS`: orgs with walkers placed on lattice cells off the grid, along the road to the lake (Prometheus Foundation, OSI, Anthem Foundation, Ayn Rand Society, Ayn Rand Centre UK).
- Galt's Gulch: the city is Atlantis, the hidden valley of Atlas Shrugged. A gold "Galt's Gulch / Atlantis" sign hangs over the mountains behind the lake (always drawn on top, never picked).
- The gulch: a stream comes out of the mountains (rocky hills line its banks) into the lake and leaves it again, running behind the outskirts and along the city's outer side; a green "Welcome to Atlantis / Pop. N" city sign stands on posts beside the road in, readable from both sides.
- Websites are fish: each row of the D1 `websites` table that is a site in its own right (orgs, publications, Substacks, forums, stores, personal sites; not per-person profile pages or grant sub-pages) is copied into the export's `websites` list and emitted as `websites` in the city JSON. The viewer draws one fish per site, looping round the stream at 1.2-4.5 m/s (each fish at its own constant pace) (gold organizations, orange Substacks, teal personal, green forums and blogs, pink stores, purple publications); hover or click for the title, owner and link. **Defunct** sites (dead domain, for sale, hijacked, spam takeover ...) are pale skeletons that drift more slowly and roll over; their card shows why they are defunct and links only to the archive copy, never to the dead address.
- Social media groups are cottages: each `social` row with `account_type` group or community (Meetup, Facebook, Reddit, LinkedIn, Goodreads, Google Groups) is emitted as `groups`. The viewer places one cottage per group: Meetup groups on the gulch floor beside the streams and the lake, the others up the mountainsides (Reddit highest). roofs come in many colors (red is reserved for Peikoff properties, so there are no red roofs unless a group belongs to a Peikoff building), the door color is the platform, size = member count; hover or click for platform, place, members and link. Facebook and LinkedIn groups have no public address in the database, so they show no link.
- Music (optional, off by default): tick "Music" in the panel. With no files it plays a waltz composed live in the browser (`public/atlas/music.js`, original, no recording). To use real recordings, put audio files in `public/atlas/audio/` and list them in `tracks.json` (or try one with `?track=file.mp3`); only use music you have the right to use.
- Cards never show confidence labels (verified, reported, ...) or machine-written bylines ("Byline on N article(s) in D1 ..."); the role and relationship text stays. Cato, Adam Smith Institute, CEVRO, FEE, Goldwater, PragerU and Students For Liberty are in `EXCLUDE_ORGS` (Atlas Network stays). The mountain ring leaves a pass along the inflow so the gulch opens behind the lake.
- No overlaps: at load the viewer pushes apart anyone closer than 1.1 m (same building and floor, or both on the street); a walker who arrives where someone stands steps clear.
- Hair: people with a feminine first name get the long-hair curtain (`city.hair`); Amy Peikoff's is a blend of brown and gray (`HAIR_OVERRIDE` in `main.js`, after the photo on her card). Barbara Branden Peikoff has her own champagne-blonde, full bouffant (regalia kind `bouffant`, after the first photo on barbarapeikoff.com).
- Easter egg: Kira Peikoff, an NPC princess, sits on the summit of the furthest tall mountain writing a book (name label above her). She lives only in the scenery (no data row), so she is not searchable, selectable or hoverable. How movement works, and where the shapes come from: see `MOVEMENT.md`.
- Kira's smile: the Princess Peach model's mouth is a small open "o" (pink lips around a black cavity). `smileMouth()` in `public/atlas/kira-face.js` reshapes those vertices when the model loads: lips and cavity are squeezed into one band, widened, flattened against the face and curved up at the corners, and the cavity takes the lip colour. Nothing is drawn over her face (an earlier version put a separate red arc over the "o", which looked like a stray line beside her real mouth).
- Editorial decisions (exclusions, merges, scrubbed notes, website curation) are recorded in `decisions.json` and enforced by `apply_decisions.py`, which both makers run first, so a data refresh cannot undo them. See `DECISIONS.md` (what is protected, the refresh runbook, the limits).
- Scenery (viewer only, `?scenery=0` hides it): a ring of low-poly mountains far out and a lake between the city and them; colors follow the day/night theme.
- Ayn Rand is drawn as herself: short dark bob with straight bangs, dark eyes and brows, black dress with dark stockings, a gold dollar-sign brooch and a cigarette holder (plus the crown). Regalia kinds `hair`, `face`, `dress`, `dollarpin`, `holder`.
- `ROUTES_ONLY` replaces a person's walking route (Carl Barney: Prometheus Foundation, OSI, The Objective Standard); profile links list non-Wikipedia links before Wikipedia.

## Speaking with people (paid; admin testing)

Press and hold a person (a long press, which is what a force press is on a current iPhone), double click / double tap, or right-click: a small menu opens at the finger or pointer. Its first item depends on who you are: the admin (or a paid user) gets **Chat with <name>**, a visitor who is not signed in gets **Sign up to chat with <name>** ("Talk with the people of the Atlas. Registration is opening soon."), and a signed-in user without a paid plan gets **Upgrade to chat with <name>**; everyone also gets **Show details** (the person's card). A single tap still selects the person. The chat panel is a conversation where the model plays that person. The system prompt (`personaPrompt` in `src/chat.js`) casts it as a character in the Atlas, a virtual world that represents Objectivism, handed the character's card (profile fields and named relationships, no links) and told to be that person and never break character: no mention of prompts, models or role-play, and in-world answers to "what are you?". It must not invent private details or quotations, and never claims to be the real person or a human. The panel itself still shows a one-line notice that this is an AI simulation. Conversations are kept per person until the page reloads ("New" starts over).

On a phone the first tap of a double tap opens the person's card over the spot where they stand, so the second tap lands on the card; that counts as the double tap. Touch and pen taps also snap to the nearest visible person within 28 px (a fingertip is wider than a person), and a finger may wobble 12 px and still count as a tap (a mouse 5 px).

- **Admin pass (for testing the paid mode):** type `/admin` in the search box (or open `/atlas/?admin`) and enter the admin password. That is the Worker secret `ADMIN_PASSWORD`, or `USER_PASSWORD` if `ADMIN_PASSWORD` is not set. It sets a signed, HttpOnly session cookie for 30 days; `/logout` signs out, and changing the password signs every session out. Both commands are unlisted.
- **Model (provider-neutral):** `POST /api/chat` makes one OpenAI-style `POST {base}/chat/completions` call with the Worker secret `LLM_KEY` (plain `fetch`, no SDK; OpenAI, Mistral and xAI all accept it). `LLM_PROVIDER` (`openai` default, `mistral`, `xai`) picks the base URL and a default model; `LLM_MODEL` overrides the model; `LLM_BASE_URL` points at any other OpenAI-compatible endpoint (then `LLM_MODEL` is required). Only `model` and `messages` are sent, because token-limit and temperature parameters differ between providers and models. A refusal or content-filter stop comes back as an in-character decline.
- **API:** `GET /api/me`, `POST /api/admin/login` `{ password }`, `POST /api/logout`, `POST /api/chat` `{ personId, messages: [{ role, content }] }` (user first and last, alternating, at most 40 messages of 4000 characters). 401 = not signed in, 402 = signed in but not paid. These four are also served on the `atlas.` host; nothing else under `/api/` is.
- **Accounts (Supabase)** (`src/accounts.js`, `supabase/migrations/20261016000000_accounts_and_chat_cache.sql`): visitors sign up with an email link (no password) or Google, through Supabase Auth. The menu's "Sign up to chat" opens the dialog; Supabase sends the person back to `/atlas/` with tokens in the address, the page hands them to `POST /api/auth/session`, and the Worker keeps them in two HttpOnly cookies (`atlas_at`, an hour; `atlas_rt`, 30 days; renewed automatically). Who may chat comes from the user's `profiles.plan` (`free` | `paid`, optional `paid_until`): free accounts get the paid-plan notice (402); users cannot change their own plan (billing will, later). The admin session (`/admin`) still works and takes precedence. API: `POST /api/auth/otp { email }`, `GET /api/auth/google`, `POST /api/auth/session { access_token, refresh_token }`; `/api/me` now returns `{ user, accounts }`.
  - **Sign-in screen shows the Supabase URL?** Google's consent screen names the host of the OAuth callback. To show `auth.peikoff.net` instead, enable a Supabase custom domain on `auth.peikoff.net` (paid add-on, about $10/month, Pro plan), add `https://auth.peikoff.net/auth/v1/callback` to the Google and GitHub OAuth apps (keep the old `supabase.co` callback too), activate it, then set the Worker variable `SUPABASE_URL` to `https://auth.peikoff.net`. No code change: every call here builds its URLs from `SUPABASE_URL`.
  - **Setup:** (1) apply the migration to your Supabase project; (2) in Supabase, Authentication > URL Configuration: add `https://peikoff.net/atlas/` and `https://atlas.peikoff.net/` as redirect URLs (and set the Site URL to `https://peikoff.net/atlas/`), and turn on the Email and Google providers; (3) set the Worker variables `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` (the public publishable key), and the Worker secret `SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY` (only for the shared cache below). To make someone paid: `update public.profiles set plan = 'paid' where id = '<user id>';`, or put their email in `public.paid_emails` before they sign up (they start on the paid plan; the owner's email is in it already).
- **Usage caps** (`src/usage.js`, `supabase/migrations/2026101614*.sql`, `...15*.sql`): every paid user has three limits per calendar month (UTC), **whichever comes first**: **100 questions** put to the model (`CHAT_MONTHLY_QUESTIONS`), **2,000,000 tokens** counting the prompt and the answer (`CHAT_MONTHLY_TOKENS`), and a dollar budget of **$1.50 a month and $0.30 a day** (`CHAT_MONTHLY_BUDGET_USD`, `CHAT_DAILY_BUDGET_USD`), which is the safety net that holds whatever the model costs. At $5 a month Stripe keeps about $0.48 (2.9% + $0.30, plus 0.7% for Stripe Billing), leaving about $4.50, so a user who uses every cent still leaves about $3.00 for Supabase, Cloudflare and profit. After each call the cost and tokens are counted from the usage the provider reports (tokens times the model's price per million, `PRICES` in `usage.js`; an unknown model is priced high on purpose; `LLM_PRICE_IN`/`LLM_PRICE_OUT` override); before each call the worst case (the text sent at 3 characters per token plus the longest answer) is estimated and the call is refused (429, e.g. "You have used your 100 questions for this month. They come back on 2026-11-01.") if it would not fit. Answers are limited to `CHAT_MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS` (500). Cache hits cost nothing and are not counted; the admin is not capped. It fails closed: without `SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY`, or if usage cannot be read, capped users get a 503 rather than free chat. Replies carry `allowance.used_pct` and `questions_left`. Spend is in the table `chat_usage` (`select user_id, sum(cost_micro) / 1e6 as usd, sum(messages) as questions, sum(tokens) as tokens from chat_usage group by 1`). The default model's prices (gpt-4o-mini: $0.15 in / $0.60 out per million tokens) are in the table from memory: check your provider's price list when you change the model.
- **Chat log** (`src/chatlog.js`, `supabase/migrations/20261016160000_chat_log.sql`): every question and answer is written to `public.chat_log` (who, email, which person, turn number, text, cached, refused, model, cost in micro-dollars), best effort and in the background so it never slows or breaks a chat; admin chats are logged with no user id. The table has row level security and no policy: only the Worker (service key) and the project owner read it. Retention: after 90 days `public.chat_log_prune(90)` (run daily at 03:17 UTC by pg_cron, job `chat-log-prune`) blanks the question, answer, email and user id and sets `scrubbed_at`, keeping only the anonymous statistics (time, person, turn, cached, refused, model, cost). The privacy page (`/privacy`) says so. Read it in Supabase, Table editor, `chat_log`, or with SQL such as `select created_at, email, person_name, question from chat_log order by id desc limit 50`.
- **HubSpot contacts** (`src/hubspot.js`): everyone who signs in is upserted into HubSpot by email (name, email, lifecycle stage `subscriber`) and a Stripe checkout moves the payer to `customer`. Needs the Worker secret `HUBSPOT_TOKEN` (HubSpot, Settings, Integrations, Private apps, scope `crm.objects.contacts.write`); without it nothing happens. Best effort and in the background. News and updates go only to people who opt in (the privacy page says so).
- **Caching across users** (`src/cache.js`): (1) the first question of a conversation is looked up before the model is called, per character and per version of the prompt, after normalizing it ("Who ARE you?!" = "who are you"); a hit costs nothing and is instant, from the Worker's memory or the shared Supabase table `chat_cache` (14 days; the admin always gets a fresh answer; follow-up turns are never cached; errors and refusals never stored). Editing `src/personas.js` or a card changes the key, so the cache refreshes itself. (2) The provider's own prompt caching: the system prompt is built with the part that is the same for everybody first (the admin's extra lines last), and OpenAI is sent a `prompt_cache_key` per character, so a repeated long prefix is billed at the cached rate; other providers cache a repeated prefix on their own.
- **Sign in with Google, Apple, GitHub and more** (`GET /api/auth/<provider>`; providers: google, apple, github, microsoft, facebook, twitter, linkedin, discord, gitlab, bitbucket, slack, spotify, twitch, notion, figma, zoom; the Worker variable `AUTH_PROVIDERS` is a comma list of the ones to show (or `all`); default `google,github`; enable each in Supabase, Authentication, Providers, with callback `https://<project>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`): all go through Supabase Auth, same flow as the email link. Setup is in the providers, not in code: (1) Google: in Google Cloud, create an OAuth client (Web) whose redirect URI is `https://<project>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`, then enter its client ID and secret in Supabase, Authentication, Providers, Google. (2) Apple: needs the Apple Developer Program; create an App ID and a Services ID (the client ID) with Sign in with Apple on, add the same callback URL and your domain, create a key, generate the client secret (a JWT Apple lets live at most 6 months, so it must be regenerated twice a year) and enter them in Supabase, Providers, Apple.
- **Monetization with Stripe** (`src/billing.js`, `supabase/migrations/20261016130000_stripe_billing.sql`): a paid plan is a Stripe subscription. "Upgrade to chat" opens a Subscribe dialog; Subscribe goes to Stripe Checkout (`POST /api/billing/checkout`) and back to `/atlas/?billing=success`; `/billing` (typed in the search box) opens Stripe's customer portal for cards, invoices and cancelling (`POST /api/billing/portal`). The plan is turned on only by Stripe's signed webhook (`POST /api/stripe/webhook`: signature checked with 5 minutes of tolerance, each event applied once, recorded in `stripe_events` only after the profile was written so a failure is retried): `checkout.session.completed` stores the customer and subscription; `customer.subscription.created/updated` set `plan = paid` and `paid_until` = the end of the paid period (so a cancelled or failed subscription runs out by itself); `.deleted` ends it. `/api/me` reports `billing: true` when it is configured.
